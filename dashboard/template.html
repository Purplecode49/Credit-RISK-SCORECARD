Where the expected loss sits

Expected loss is PD × LGD × EAD for each loan, added up by score band. Low scores are a small part of the exposure and a large part of the loss.

PD is the scorecard PD converted to a 12-month figure. LGD is assumed at 65% for cash loans and 75% for revolving loans, plus 5 points for the largest quarter of loans. EAD is the full credit amount at origination for cash loans, and limit × (50% drawn + 50% of the undrawn half × 75% CCF) for revolving loans. The loan term used for the 12-month conversion comes from the annuity at an assumed 22% interest rate.

Stress test

Each scenario lowers every score, which raises PD (20 points doubles the odds of default), and adds to LGD and to the share of revolving limits drawn.

If the LGD and CCF assumptions are wrong

12-month expected loss in millions with today's PDs. LGD low and high are 15 points below and above the central case. CCF is the share of the unused revolving limit that gets drawn before default (60%, 75%, 90%). The LGD assumption matters far more than the CCF.
